# Form in Music: With Special Reference to the Designs of Instrumental Music
**Author:** Stewart Macpherson, F.R.A.M.

## Chapter I: The Elements of Musical Form: Motive, Phrase, and Period

Form in music represents the architectural organization of sound in time. Just as architectural design requires balance, proportion, symmetry, and variety, musical composition relies upon intelligible formal structure.

### 1. The Motive
The motive (or motif) is the smallest recognizable structural unit of musical expression. It usually consists of two or three notes possessing a distinct rhythmic and melodic contour (e.g., the four-note opening motive of Beethoven's Fifth Symphony).

### 2. The Phrase
A phrase is a musical thought that typically extends across four measures (bars) in standard classical rhythm, though phrases of two, three, five, or six measures occur. A phrase concludes with a cadence, which provides a momentary punctuation or breathing point.

### 3. The Period (Sentence and Period Structure)
A period is formed by the combination of two complementary phrases:
1. **The Antecedent Phrase (Fore-phrase)**: Opens the thematic statement and ends with an inconclusive cadence, most often a Half Cadence (HC) on the dominant or an Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC). This leaves the musical thought incomplete, creating a musical question.
2. **The Consequent Phrase (After-phrase)**: Answers the antecedent, often repeating the opening motive, but concluding with a conclusive Perfect Authentic Cadence (PAC) on the tonic. This completes the musical statement.

## Chapter II: Fundamental Instrumental Forms

### 1. Binary Form (Two-Part Form, AB)
Binary form consists of two balanced sections:
- **Part A**: Begins in the tonic key and modulates, ending with a cadence in the dominant key (or relative major if the piece is in a minor key).
- **Part B**: Begins in the dominant/relative key and works its way back to the tonic key, closing with a strong authentic cadence in the home tonic.
Both sections are frequently marked with repeat signs (||: A :||: B :||).

### 2. Ternary Form (Three-Part Form, ABA)
Ternary form consists of three distinct sections:
- **Part A (Statement)**: Complete thematic idea in the home tonic, ending with a conclusive cadence.
- **Part B (Contrast / Digression)**: Offers contrast in melodic material, texture, or key (often the subdominant, dominant, or relative minor).
- **Part A (Restatement / Return)**: Exact or varied reprise of the first section in the original tonic key, followed by an optional coda.
Examples include the Minuet and Trio, Scherzo, and classical song forms.

### 3. Rondo Form (ABACA or ABACABA)
Rondo form is based on the recurrent alternation of a principal theme (the refrain or ritornello, A) with contrasting episodes (B, C, etc.). The recurring A section always returns in the principal tonic key.
