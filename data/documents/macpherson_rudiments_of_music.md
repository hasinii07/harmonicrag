## Section I: Pitch, Staves, and Clefs

Music is the art of sound. Pitch denotes the degree of highness or lowness of a sound, determined by frequency of vibration. Sounds are represented graphically on a five-line stave or staff.

### 1. Clefs
The clef fixes the pitch of a particular line on the staff:
1. **The Treble Clef (G Clef)**: Fixes the pitch G4 above middle C on the second line of the staff. Used for high instruments and soprano/alto voices.
2. **The Bass Clef (F Clef)**: Fixes the pitch F3 below middle C on the fourth line of the staff. Used for lower instruments and bass/tenor voices.
3. **The C Clefs**: Fix middle C (C4). The **Alto Clef** places C4 on the third line (used for viola); the **Tenor Clef** places C4 on the fourth line (used for cello and bassoon in higher registers).

## Section II: Tones, Semitones, and Scales

The smallest interval commonly employed in Western music theory is the **semitone** (half-step). A **tone** (whole-step) consists of two semitones.

### 1. The Diatonic Major Scale
A diatonic scale is an alphabetical sequence of seven pitch classes within an octave. The Major Scale is formed by five tones and two semitones, arranged in the invariant pattern:
**Tone – Tone – Semitone – Tone – Tone – Tone – Semitone** (or W – W – H – W – W – W – H).
Semitones occur naturally between the 3rd and 4th degrees, and between the 7th and 8th degrees.

### 2. Minor Scales
The minor mode exists in three theoretical forms:
1. **Natural Minor (Pure or Aeolian)**: Formed by the step pattern Tone – Semitone – Tone – Tone – Semitone – Tone – Tone (1 – 2 – b3 – 4 – 5 – b6 – b7).
2. **Harmonic Minor**: Formed by raising the 7th degree of the natural minor by one semitone, both ascending and descending (1 – 2 – b3 – 4 – 5 – b6 – 7). This creates an augmented second interval between the 6th and 7th degrees (e.g., Ab to B in A minor), providing a true leading tone.
3. **Melodic Minor**: Ascending, the 6th and 7th degrees are both raised (1 – 2 – b3 – 4 – 5 – 6 – 7) to smooth voice leading; descending, the 6th and 7th degrees revert to natural minor (b7 and b6).

## Section III: Intervals and Semitone Counts

An interval is the difference in pitch between two notes. Intervals are reckoned upward from the lower note.

### 1. Specific Interval Classification
1. **Unison**: Perfect Unison (0 semitones).
2. **Seconds**: Minor Second (1 semitone), Major Second (2 semitones), Augmented Second (3 semitones).
3. **Thirds**: Minor Third (3 semitones), Major Third (4 semitones).
4. **Fourths**: Diminished Fourth (4 semitones), Perfect Fourth (5 semitones), Augmented Fourth (6 semitones).
5. **Fifths**: Diminished Fifth (6 semitones), Perfect Fifth (7 semitones), Augmented Fifth (8 semitones).
6. **Sixths**: Minor Sixth (8 semitones), Major Sixth (9 semitones).
7. **Sevenths**: Diminished Seventh (9 semitones), Minor Seventh (10 semitones), Major Seventh (11 semitones).
8. **Octave**: Perfect Octave (12 semitones).

### 2. The Tritone
An interval consisting of three whole tones (6 semitones) is termed a **Tritone**. Historically known as *diabolus in musica*, the tritone occurs between the 4th and 7th degrees of the major scale (e.g., F to B in C major). It is represented either as an Augmented Fourth (F to B) or as a Diminished Fifth (B to F). While enharmonically sounding the same interval, they resolve in opposite directions: the augmented fourth expands outward (F falls to E, B rises to C), while the diminished fifth contracts inward (B rises to C, F falls to E).

## Section IV: Rhythm and Time Signatures: Simple vs. Compound Meter

Meter governs the regular grouping of beats in musical time.

### 1. Simple Time (Simple Meter)
In simple time, each main beat naturally divides into **two equal parts** (duple subdivision).
- **Simple Duple**: 2/4 (two quarter-note beats per bar, each dividing into two eighth notes).
- **Simple Triple**: 3/4 (three quarter-note beats per bar, each dividing into two eighth notes).
- **Simple Quadruple**: 4/4 or Common Time C (four quarter-note beats per bar).

### 2. Compound Time (Compound Meter)
In compound time, each main beat is a dotted note that naturally divides into **three equal parts** (triple subdivision).
- **Compound Duple**: 6/8. It consists of **two dotted-quarter beats per bar**, each dividing into three eighth notes. It is NOT simple triple meter.
- **Compound Triple**: 9/8. Three dotted-quarter beats per bar, dividing into nine eighth notes.
- **Compound Quadruple**: 12/8. Four dotted-quarter beats per bar, dividing into twelve eighth notes.
