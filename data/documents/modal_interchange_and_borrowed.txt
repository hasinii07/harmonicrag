Modal interchange (also called mode mixture) is a harmonic technique where chords from a parallel mode (typically the parallel minor) are borrowed and used within the primary mode (typically the major key). The most common borrowed chord in major keys is the minor subdominant triad (iv) and minor subdominant seventh chord (iv7), such as F minor (F-Ab-C) in C major, which introduces the distinctive flat-sixth scale degree (Le, Ab) resolving downward to scale degree 5 (Sol, G). Another ubiquitous borrowed chord is the flat-submediant major chord (bVI, Ab-C-Eb in C major) and the flat-seventh major triad (bVII, Bb-D-F in C major), commonly termed the subtonic chord. These borrowed chords add emotional darkness and harmonic color without modulating away from the tonic center.
